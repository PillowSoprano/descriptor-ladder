# When does a contrast-level split stop testing anything?

_Generated by `scripts/run_leakage_census.py`._ A held-out contrast leaks when both of its constituent units survive into the training set through some other contrast. That is a property of the contrast graph and the split, so it can be computed from the shape of a design before the design is run. Test fraction $q=0.2$ throughout, 8 draws.

Conditional on the contrast being scored falling in the test fold, each endpoint reaches training only through its *other* contrasts, so a unit of degree $d$ is absent with probability $q^{d-1}$ and the contrast leaks with probability $(1-q^{d_u-1})(1-q^{d_v-1})$. The exponent is $d-1$ and not $d$: a unit of degree one is certainly absent, and the version without the conditioning overstates the leak rate on sparse graphs by about fifteen points. Nothing about the science enters anywhere. The simulation column is what checks that this is right rather than plausible.

The leak rate passes 99\% at about 5 contrasts per unit, and it is above 90\% well before that. Any response or difference benchmark with more contrasts than a few per unit, which is nearly all of them, tests reconstruction rather than generalisation if the split is made on contrasts.

## What this does and does not claim

It does not claim that any published study made this split. Auditing that would need each study's own split code and is not attempted here. What it establishes is weaker in one way and stronger in another: for a contrast graph of a given shape, a contrast-level split leaks at a rate that follows from the shape alone, and the threshold above which it leaks almost everything is low enough that most such graphs are past it. A reader can therefore check their own design against the table without running anything, which is the point.
