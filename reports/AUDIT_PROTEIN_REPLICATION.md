## What replicates, and what does not

**Replicates.**

- The basis is a real cost, and a large one. At the densest design the descriptor restriction is +62\% worse than free effects, against $+26$ and $+21$\% on the perovskite grid at 90\% coverage. The exact ANOVA agrees with the fitted comparison: the descriptor span captures 56\% of the true main effects, which sits between the perovskite grid's 50.4 and 78.4\%.
- The metric's value decays as the design ratio grows, which is the shape the coverage law asserts. It peaks at +7.9\% at $n/p=0.78$ and is -0.6\% at $n/p=7.3$.
- Free effects beat the descriptor basis in 1516 of 1759 configurations (86\%), at every design ratio in the sweep.

**Does not replicate.**

- There is no interpolation peak. The free arm's error is flat to within 3\% below $n/p=1$ and falls monotonically above it, where the perovskite grid rises 31\% into a peak at $n/p\approx1$. The mechanism explains the difference rather than excusing it: on a two-factor product a second-order model is saturated, so the class compared here is additive and carries no interaction block to be weakly identified. The peak is a property of designs whose parameters are mostly interaction parameters, not of categorical products as such.
- The trap the perovskite grid sets is absent. There, the basis appears to win by 17--19\% in the band around $n/p=1$ because the free arm is momentarily at its worst. Here the basis cost is positive at every ratio, from +36 to +127\%, and never changes sign.

**New here.**

- The metric has a window rather than a monotone decay to zero. It helps between $n/p\approx0.5$ and $1.2$ and *hurts* outside it, by -6.3\% at the sparsest design and by -0.6\% at the densest. The smoothing weight is a fixed constant in both this and the perovskite sweep, so what changes is not tuning: with almost nothing observed, pulling each level toward its descriptor neighbours imports the basis's bias without buying any variance reduction worth having.
- Gradient boosting on the same descriptors is exactly the zero baseline below $n/p=1$, to four decimal places, because it makes no splits on that little data. It overtakes the free arm only at the densest design (0.4405 against 0.4448).
